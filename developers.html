Why builders bring us in early

Retaining walls and dirt are among the biggest swings in what a finished lot costs. The time to control them is before the grading is set.

Lower finished lot costFewer and shorter walls and a balanced site lower the cost of every lot before the first pad is cut.
Wall costs that holdWalls designed to a reviewed grading plan, with global stability settled up front, keep their price from bid to final.
Your civil keeps the siteWe check and recommend; your civil engineer stays the site designer. We work inside your process and your schedule.

Send it at 30%, not 90%

A 30% design is easy to change. A 90% design is not. Bring us in early and we help steer the grading and wall layout so the final civil set is already optimized and the walls are ready to design.

30% designThe best time to callPad grades, pond depths, street profiles and wall layout can still move. Most of the savings are found here.
60% designStill worth itUtilities are laid out and changes take more coordination, but grading and walls can still be tuned.
90% designMostly locked inChanges mean redesign, resubmittal and schedule. What is left is designing walls to the plan as drawn.

Land under contract? A concept plan and topo are enough for a grading and wall check during due diligence, so the wall and dirt cost is known before closing.

What we check before we design the walls

Grading sets the wall heights, and the storm and sewer set how far the grades can move. We look at all three together.

01 · Retaining walls

Wall layout and heights

The walls on the grading plan, checked for height, type and stability before anyone prices them.

  • Wall face reduced by adjusting grades
  • Wall type by height and lot condition
  • Global stability from the geotech, including tiered and pond walls
  • Conflicts with lots, easements and utilities flagged early
02 · Grading

Grading and earthwork

Pad, street and pond grades checked against the dirt balance and the walls they create.

  • Pad and street grades adjusted to cut fill and wall height
  • Site raised or lowered a few tenths where it pays
  • Ponds deepened or reshaped to supply fill
  • Dirt balanced by phase instead of hauled off and back
03 · Utilities

Utility checks for grading

Outfall elevations, pipe cover and sewer depths set how far a site can be raised or lowered.

  • Outfall and tailwater elevations checked against a lowered site
  • Storm inverts raised where they run deep
  • Minimum cover and sewer depth limits on street and pad grades
  • Lift station and force main phasing checked against grading

What you receive

A clear review, then the wall design

The review opens with the site as drawn against the site optimized. It then lists the plan changes that get you there, written for your civil engineer to adopt.

Once the grading is settled, we design the walls to it and deliver sealed plans for permit and construction. Tall and tiered walls get a global stability analysis before they are priced.

About the firm

Questions developers ask

Do you replace our civil engineer?

No. Your civil engineer designs the site and stays the engineer of record for it. We review the plans, recommend changes, and design and seal the retaining walls.

When should we send plans?

As early as you have a concept or 30% grading plan. Changes are easy at 30% and get harder at every submittal after that.

What do you need to start?

The grading plan, the utility layout if there is one, and the geotech report if it is available. CAD files help, but PDFs are fine.

Our plans are already at 90%. Is it too late?

Not for the walls. We can still check wall layout, type and global stability before bid and design the walls to the plans as drawn.

How is the work priced?

We quote a fee for the review and the wall design up front from your plan set.
